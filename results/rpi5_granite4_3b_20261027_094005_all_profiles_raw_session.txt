*Definition*: A transformer is an electrical device that transfers electrical energy between two or more circuits through electromagnetic induction, changing voltage levels according to its design.

**3. High-Voltage Transmission**

After generation, electricity travels from power plants to substations via high-voltage transmission lines. These lines have very high voltages (typically 200,000-765,000 volts) to minimize energy loss during transmission.

*Analogy*: You can think of these high-voltage lines as superhighways for electricity, where the electricity is packed into a powerful stream that can travel long distances with minimal loss.

**4. Substations**

*Definition*: A substation is a part of an electrical generation, transmission, and distribution system where voltage levels are transformed from high to medium or low using transformers.

**5. Distribution Networks**

Low-voltage electricity then flows through distribution lines to your neighborhood. These lines have much lower voltages (typically 4,000-33,000 volts).
